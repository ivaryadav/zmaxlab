# ZmaxLab Brand Guidelines
**Status:** v1 — established in Phase 3 (2026-08-08)

## Mission
Give solo and small-practice healthcare providers a website that looks and functions like it was built by a real team — without the agency price tag, contract, or wait time.

## Vision
To be the default first website for independent NPs and small healthcare practices launching or relaunching their online presence — known for being fast, fairly priced, and genuinely custom.

## Brand Story
ZmaxLab is built and run by one person who codes every site personally, rather than assembling one from a template library or outsourcing it. That's a constraint (limited throughput, one point of failure) as much as it is a selling point, and the brand should be honest about both sides of that rather than only leaning on the flattering half.

## Core Values
1. **Honesty over hype.** Every claim on the site should be something Ravi could defend if a prospect asked "how do you know that?" No invented statistics, no manufactured urgency.
2. **Speed with substance.** 7-day delivery is a real commitment, not a gimmick — it should be paired with evidence of process (how the 7 days are actually spent), not just repeated as a slogan.
3. **Plain-spoken expertise.** Healthcare buyers are used to being marketed to aggressively; the brand differentiates by sounding like a competent specialist explaining things clearly, not a salesperson.
4. **Fair, simple pricing.** $500 flat, no contract — stated plainly, not buried or "starting at."

## Brand Personality
Competent, direct, calm, specific. Not: hyper-energetic, salesy, jargon-heavy, or trying to look bigger than it is. Think "the specialist a colleague recommends," not "the agency running ads at you."

## Tone of Voice
- Write like a specialist explaining something to a smart peer, not like an ad.
- Prefer concrete, checkable claims ("$500 flat fee, delivered in 7 business days") over vague superlatives ("best," "revolutionary," "game-changing").
- Never fabricate numbers, client outcomes, or urgency. If a claim can't be sourced, cut it or reframe it as illustrative and label it as such.
- Avoid exclamation points and hype adjectives ("amazing," "incredible," "dominate"). Confidence should come from specificity, not intensity.
- It's fine to acknowledge limitations plainly (e.g., solo-founder capacity) rather than paper over them — this fits the "honesty over hype" value and actually builds more trust with a skeptical healthcare buyer.

## Messaging Framework
- **Primary message:** Custom-coded healthcare websites, $500 flat, live in 7 business days — built personally, not templated or outsourced.
- **Supporting message 1 (price/value):** Meaningfully cheaper than both direct competitors (~$1,100+) and full-service agencies (~$5,000+), without sacrificing custom build quality.
- **Supporting message 2 (speed):** Most competitors don't publish a delivery timeline at all; ZmaxLab's 7-day commitment is a stated, comparable advantage.
- **Supporting message 3 (founder model):** One specialist building your site personally is a meaningful trust signal when framed honestly — direct communication, direct accountability, no account-manager layer.

## Unique Value Proposition (UVP)
"A fully custom healthcare website, built personally by a specialist, for a flat $500, delivered in 7 business days — no contract, no template."

## Differentiators (ranked by defensibility)
1. Price + no contract (provable today, easiest to defend)
2. Delivery speed (provable today, differentiated since competitors don't publish timelines)
3. Founder-built model (true today, but a capacity constraint worth stating honestly rather than only marketing)
4. Healthcare-specific credibility signals — NPI verification / HIPAA-aware language (currently illustrative in the site mockup; becomes a real differentiator only once operationalized as an actual part of the process, not just a badge)

## What NOT to do (guardrails, directly from user direction)
- No manufactured urgency ("only 3 spots left," countdowns, fake scarcity).
- No fabricated or unverifiable percentage claims about client results.
- No "salesy" copy or over-exaggeration — plain, defensible language only.
- No implying real client testimonials/logos are verified until they actually are.
